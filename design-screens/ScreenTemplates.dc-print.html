Type

Inter is the only typeface. Hierarchy comes from weight and tracking, never from a change of face.

Page titles 30–44px at 600. Headings 21–26px at 600. Body 14px at 400.

Tracking tightens as size grows: −0.02em at 21px, −0.03em at 44px and above.

Money, references and counts use tabular figures, so columns align.

Elevation

Three levels. 0 flat on the ground, hairline only. 1 the card. 2 floats over the page — header, tab bar, menus. 3 covers it — sheets, drawers, modals.

Soft, low-spread, warm ink at 4–12%. No hard drop shadows.

Every level pairs a shadow with a hairline. On a small contrast step an edge reads before a shadow does.

Geometry

Card radius 14px. Controls 6px. Panels 8px. 8px spacing base.

Touch targets never below 44px.

Content maxes at 1280px — margins grow, content does not. 768px is the shell switch.

Currency is always MWK 85,000 — grouped, never a currency symbol.

The rubber stamp

A heavy, hand-pressed stamp — not a badge and not a chip. The stamps are supplied artwork, not drawn in code: a double ring carrying Ganyu Hub arced above and below, three stars each side, and an angled label band naming the state. Worn edges, uneven ink, every one different.

The five stamps mark the stages a job's money passes through. Keyed to transparent so they sit on any ground, and cut from 2048px to 512px: the set is about a tenth of the weight it arrived at, which matters on a paid connection.

Ink, and the rules

Nothing yet belongs to empty states and nowhere else. The five money stamps are never borrowed for absence, because they name stages of money and nothing has moved yet.

Sizes: 80px in dense rows, 104px on mobile, 148px from md up.

Never redraw, approximate, or substitute a chip, badge or dashed border.

The money header

The money leads. Eyebrow, then the amount at 27px in tabular figures, then the stamp pressed at the right margin on its light-grey ground. Nothing sits above it.

The stamp is 104px here and 148px from md up. It overhangs the padding slightly, as a pressed stamp would.

The fee breakdown is an inset panel — #F1F1F0 with a #DCDCDC hairline, inside the raised card. Three rows: agreed price, processing fee, what you actually paid. Always say who charges what.

Mobile pins the action. A sticky bar at the bottom repeats the state in words and carries one teal button. It throws its shadow upward.

What the job card owes the reader

Radius 14px, level 1, hairline border. On the small ground-to-card step the border does as much work as the shadow.

The client's trust signals sit above the money — who posted it, that they have paid into escrow before, how many jobs they have posted. A creative deciding whether to bid is deciding whether this person pays.

Budget in the reserved green at chip scale. #2F5D3B is money-adjacent only: budget pills and the verified badge. It is never a general accent.

The category pill uses the accent — light grey at chip scale, which is where the accent works hardest.

Proposal count only when there are proposals. A job with none shows the empty state, not “0 proposals”.

The teal rail on the left edge is 4px and marks an open job.

The quiet weight — one empty region on a page that is otherwise full. No panel, no button.

Two weights. A whole empty surface gets the stamp, a sentence explaining what will fill it, and a way out. One empty region on a page that is otherwise full gets a line of text and nothing else.

The dashed edge is 2px #DCDCDC — the same hairline the inset panels use, so absence reads as an unstamped document rather than an error.

Say what will fill it. “Creatives usually reply within a day” is a fact about the market. Never “Nothing to see here”.

The way out is outlined, not teal. An empty state is not the place for the page's primary action.
